Event 3 Report: Applied Agentic AI and Practical Hackathon Experience

Summary Report: Applied Agentic AI and Practical Hackathon Experience

1. Event Objectives

  • Experience the journey of plunging in, facing intense time pressure, and cultivating a pragmatic product development (MVP) mindset through the heat of the Agentic AI Build Week Hackathon.
  • Discover the breakthrough power of Agentic AI in solving real-world enterprise operational problems, ranging from crowd control and multi-channel food ordering to strategic risk analysis and cloud architecture design assistance.

2. Guest Speakers (Hackathon Teams)

  • Team 3KA (Huynh An Khuong, Nguyen Quoc Huy, Ngo Quang Khoi, Hoang Le Thanh Duc, Dang Nguyen Phuoc Loc, Dang Truong Hung) - Project S.H.E.P.H.E.R.D (Crowd monitoring and forecasting).
  • One Team (Anh Duy, Tran Dong, Doan Trung, Minh Viet, Anshul Roy) - Project KFC Bot Agent (Multi-channel ordering assistant).
  • Plan V (Pham Tien Thuan Phat, Huynh Hoang Long, Le Minh Nghia, Tran Dai Vi, Nguyen An) - Project SA Professional AI Native App (Agent assisting cloud architects).
  • Team Signal Scout (Le Tan Luc, Do Hoang Hieu, Trieu Quoc Hao, Nguyen Van Duy Khiem, Nguyen Cong Minh, Nguyen Tran Minh Quan) - Project Signal Scout (Enterprise risk analysis system).

3. Key Highlights

The Agentic AI Breakthrough: When AI Autonomous Plans and Acts

Agentic AI marks a revolutionary paradigm shift where AI is no longer merely passively responding. Instead, it has the capability to autonomously plan, invoke business tools/APIs to solve complex problems, and independently verify results.

This was vividly demonstrated through the KFC Bot Agent—a system that helps customers order food directly via Zalo or Messenger without switching apps or dealing with tedious registrations. Even more impressive was the SA Professional app, which could automatically extract Business Requirements Document (BRD) details, draw architectural diagrams on Draw.io, and estimate AWS infrastructure costs within minutes.

The 24-Hour Hackathon Journey: Chaotic but Invaluable

The hackathon was truly a sweet and emotional “trial by fire,” taking the teams through an entire spectrum of emotions: from deep anxiety and total energy depletion to the exhilarating moment when the product finally worked.

The “laugh-or-cry” behind-the-scenes stories—internal arguments over undefined roles, staying awake until 3 AM debugging code, chugging 5 cans of Red Bull, forgetting to commit code, or accidentally pushing secret files (.env) to GitHub—left unforgettable lessons that no school or textbook could ever teach.

4. Key Takeaways

The Pragmatic MVP Mindset

“Small, finished work beats big, broken ideas.” This is the most valuable lesson for overcoming time pressure: having the courage to trim features and focusing entirely on executing one core feature flawlessly (“Scope it tiny”).

To deliver a successful 3-minute pitch to the judges, clear role division (who codes, who designs, who pitches) and meticulously preparing the demo script proved to be the decisive factors.

Flexible and Cost-Efficient Architecture

A smart system design is one that allows the product to change and scale without having to be completely rebuilt. This is achieved by leveraging adapters to connect communication channels and decoupling independent logic-processing tools.

Applying frameworks like AgentCore not only helped slash infrastructure source code by up to 60% but also maintained extremely economical operating costs (only $0.006 per order via KFC Bot, or leveraging highly elastic serverless resources for cost optimization like Project Signal Scout).

The Power of Community and Team Diversity

The greatest victory after a hackathon does not lie in the physical prizes, but in the teammates who fought shoulder-to-shoulder through the night and the lasting relationships forged within the community.

Diversity in individual strengths within a team (differing skill sets are always better than uniform skill sets) is the key to solving multi-dimensional problems under crushing time pressure.

5. Applying to Work

  • Apply the “Scope it tiny” Philosophy: Always prioritize completing small product versions (MVPs) decisively to gather real-world user feedback, avoiding the trap of designing massive but perpetually unfinished features.
  • Build Agent-Based Architectures: Design highly modular systems, allowing for easy plug-and-play integration of new automation tools or distribution channels without disrupting the core source code.
  • Proactive Cost Management: Deeply research cloud cost optimization methods based on actual resource measurement. Actively use cost estimation tools (AWS Pricing API/Calculator) right from the architectural sketching phase.
  • Prepare the Launchpad: Always proactively prepare starter templates, cloud accounts, and necessary tools to dramatically accelerate deployment speed whenever a new project suddenly arises.

6. Event Experience

  • Vivid Experiences from Speakers: The authentic, unvarnished sharing about failures and team chaos illuminated a spark of confidence, helping me shake off the fear of “not being good enough” to take the plunge.
  • Practical Technical Exposure: Witnessing firsthand models like Amazon Bedrock and AgentCore collaborating with Computer Vision (YOLO) to create outstanding autonomous systems helped me clearly outline the technological trends of the future.
  • Smart Tool Utilization: I gained a much deeper understanding of the immense value of AI Agents in intelligently automating repetitive tasks, multiplying personal productivity while ensuring high accuracy.
  • Community Connection: The fiery, enthusiastic atmosphere and the borderless support spirit of the AWS community at the event injected me with a powerful surge of energy to continue learning and contributing.

7. Lessons Learned

  • Never wait until you feel “completely ready” before daring to sign up for practical arenas. The courage to step out of your comfort zone is the greatest launchpad.
  • An outstanding AI Agent system needs more than just a smart Large Language Model (LLM); more importantly, it requires the ability to connect precisely with a company’s reliable internal data to take practical actions.
  • Learn to love the chaos, the midnight debug errors, and the “imperfect” products. Because that is precisely where the most profound lessons and priceless memories of an engineering life are kept.
